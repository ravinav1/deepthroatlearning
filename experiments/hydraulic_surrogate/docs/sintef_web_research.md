# SINTEF source research and the next physical diagnostic

Research owner: `tcn_audit`, 2026-11-02. This is a primary-source and local-code review. It introduces no measured model gain, external target data, Kaggle score or transfer approval. The research archive is `artifacts/sintef-primary-research-001/`; the original PDFs and all failed experiments remain intact.

The strongest actionable distinction is between reproducing a solver's **full-stage relaxed commitment procedure** and improving its final physical dispatch objective. Our current features partly approximate the former, but the rejected nonlinear-PQ and physical-subset switching experiments do not implement its particular relaxed state formulation. A small fixed-head diagnostic is justified; a large cache or a claim of exact SHOP reproduction is not.

## What generated these competition inputs

The supplied `Dataset_definitions_and_explanation.pdf` explicitly names Tokke–Vinje **v5_2026_6** and **SHOP 17.17.0**. It describes 17 reservoirs, nine plants, 14 units and 168-hour cases. Legal inputs include initial reservoir volumes, complete case prices/inflows and the specified terminal water values. The runtime column is a post-solve output, not an admissible predictor. The supplied YAML contains efficiency curves, minimum/maximum power, penstock IDs and startup/shutdown constants, but no initial generator state, unit priority, `mip_flag` or `linear_startup_flag`. Their absence does not establish the commands used externally.

The separately owned source audit found a newer public generation script that reads v5 input data and uses SHOP 18.4i. It sets NoMIP, linear startup, a .5 cutoff and five full iterations; it saves `committed_out` immediately after the full solve, then performs five incremental iterations. It disables reservoir penalties. These are concrete hypotheses for the teacher, **not proof of the 17.17 competition command sequence**. The June v5 commit instead contains an older version-comparison harness. Keep the two versions separate. [Pinned public generator](https://gitlab.sintef.no/energy/open-modelling-tools/open-datasets/tokke-vinje-watercourse/-/blob/11b8d7c6e30c81c878e8a80540721d817b47a0c7/scripts/multiprocessing_dataset_generation.py)

## The formulation report changes the proposed experiment

SINTEF's *Why Optimal Solutions Differ: Mathematical Explanation of NL, MILP, and LP Hydropower Production in SHOP* describes three separate steps: H1 retains fractional switching variables; H2 replaces the PQ intercept with an origin-to-best-point segment; H3 applies the minimum-production cutoff after optimization. H3 therefore leaves the LP formulation unchanged. Full-mode commitment is fixed before incremental dispatch. Initial levels and zero reference discharge seed the first iteration; later iterations use previous beginning levels and reference flows. Missing initial state leaves the first startup uncharged. Its illustrative costs are in EUR. The tested setup is SHOP 17.9.1/v2_2026_2 without environmental constraints, so its settings cannot establish competition provenance. Its reported objective/runtime comparisons are not commitment AUC. The report gives inconsistent relative MIP gaps in different sections; no exact gap is adopted. [SINTEF report record](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/01a0f3805ef8-2645f514-8c93-4994-af5b-3f76bc65c52f/), [stable NVA record](https://nva.sikt.no/registration/01a0f3805ef8-2645f514-8c93-4994-af5b-3f76bc65c52f)

The full 17-page PDF is preserved at `artifacts/sintef-shop-formulation-report-2026.pdf`, 2,011,851 bytes, SHA256 `6eb3129ca86449809a5a049062cd1f24ce2ba4afae6649b62add688f2718ca0b`. Pages 7–9 and 11 were rendered and checked against extracted equations/tables. Illustrative test-year cases in this public report were not converted into external labels.

These findings come from the supplied topology, not inferred labels. There are no supplied minimum up/down times, ramp limits, maintenance schedules or travel-delay curves to justify inventing those constraints. Current SHOP documentation also spans later releases; use it for mechanisms, not an undocumented 17.17 default. [Release notes](https://docs.shop.sintef.energy/release-notes.html)

## Frozen diagnostic proposed from this evidence

Use the base initial-head network and its existing origin-to-best PQ. Add a continuous `u[j,t]` in `[0,1]`, linked to power and flow by the four bounds above. For internal hours only, add `s >= u[t]-u[t-1]`, `d >= u[t-1]-u[t]`; charge the supplied `startcost_const*s + stopcost_const*d`. Keep terminal conversion, routing, prices, all slacks and negative-price handling fixed. This is our explicit approximate formulation, not a verified clone of SHOP's internal model.

Compare three predeclared arms: unchanged base LP, linked-state LP with zero transition cost, and linked-state LP with the supplied costs. The zero-cost arm can restrict the base feasible region when its power/discharge minima disagree; therefore require objective equivalence only when a feasible state exists for the base solution. Report any restriction rather than silently changing limits to obtain equality. Test feasible-state bounds, cost reconstruction, balance, unit caps, free boundaries and deterministic repeated solves before reading selected labels.

Use 36 metadata-selected starts wholly before 2021: 12 evenly spaced 2015–2018, 12 in 2019 and 12 in 2020 ending by December 25. These are the same date-rule cases as the separate reachability diagnostic, not high-error cases. Freeze IDs, code/data hashes and metrics before execution. Report raw continuous power/flow/state, the descriptive .5 production cutoff, proxy AUC, mismatch and switching counts; retain all arms. Any full-cache or supervised experiment requires a separate decision based on the entire comparison. This diagnostic has no H3 feedback, no iterative head update, no integer solve and no 2021/2022 selection.

Do not repeat the rejected alternatives under a new name. One Hogga mean-head update worsened all measured groups; four damped shared-loss updates did not establish convergence (`docs/lp_head_update.md`). Physical subset switching improved plant counts but weakened unit ranking (`docs/lp_joint_switching.md`). The nonlinear-PQ/DP specialist passed 2021 and lost its locked 2022 comparison (`docs/nonlinear_pq_results.md`). Current research supports a distinct relaxed-state test while preserving those negative results.

## What the other SINTEF ML sources establish

The 2022 iScheduling talk uses hourly forecasts, initial storage and terminal values for solver-configuration learning, including overflow-MIP choices. It supports interpreting optimization settings as part of the data-generating procedure; it is not an architecture recipe or a score on this competition. [Kong presentation](https://www.sintef.no/contentassets/106baff79dc44ca5897fea4c51b1cfa7/p3_6-kong_sintef.pdf)

The 2026 InterOpt news article describes using relaxed optimization and ML to improve unit commitment. It supplies context rather than exact commands or experimental acceptance evidence. [SINTEF news](https://www.sintef.no/en/latest-news/2026/how-ai-could-squeeze-more-value-from-norwegian-hydropower/)

The official 2026 neural-comparison abstract reports simple, convolutional, recurrent and hybrid networks at 24/168 hours, F1 above 97.35%, and runtime reductions. Full architecture/training details were unavailable in the accessible record. F1 and solver runtime do not imply chronological micro-AUC, and this evidence does not justify rerunning the rejected fixed TCN. [Official paper record](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/019f93eb7024-9db6a5de-cdc7-4ad4-9d28-d051824cd633/)

The separate synthetic-data abstract describes correlated initial-state sampling and seasonal historical inputs with adjusted water values. Such scenarios still require genuine optimizer-produced labels. Resampling known cases or inventing pseudo-targets does not recreate that experiment. [Official synthetic-data record](https://www.sintef.no/en/publications/publication/019f93c0595c-4f9905ae-3c25-4b3a-8f8c-c47ac61f6eb8/)
