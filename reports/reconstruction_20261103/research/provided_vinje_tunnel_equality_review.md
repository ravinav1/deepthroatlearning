For nonzero reference difference D0, let f(D)=c*sign(D)*sqrt(abs(D)), with c=.0036/sqrt(k). Its exact derivative is positive in either direction. The tangent's intercept at D=0 is f(D0)/2; consequently it can keep the old flow direction after the head changes sign. A near-zero D0 also yields a very steep tangent and severe magnitude overshoot. An origin-passing flow secant fixes this intercept but doubles the unregularized tangent slope, so it can worsen the magnitude problem and is not an adequate standalone repair.

A narrow prospective remedy is a residual backtracking step between the current schedule and its affine-LP proposal. Define R(x)=signed_tunnel_flow(x)-physical_Q(storage(x)), using the declared beginning or end convention. In a smooth, unregularized branch, the affine LP enforces R(x)+J_R(x)*direction=0. Therefore the actual residual along a sufficiently small positive step is (1-alpha)*R(x)+O(alpha^2): max/L1/L2 residual decreases locally whenever the current residual is nonzero. This supplies a mathematical reason for backtracking, rather than choosing damping from commitment scores.
