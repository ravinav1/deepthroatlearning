# Haukeli dispatch and the erased Vinje tunnel constraints

The concrete supplied-input hypothesis is that the original nine-pool LP lets Totak, Vaamarvatn, Venemo and Hyljelihyl exchange water freely. Vinje can consequently supply Vinjevatn without respecting the two supplied tunnel bottlenecks. This can lower the downstream value of Haukeli water. The diagnostic separates these four reservoirs while preserving the original fourteen generator curves, terminal conversion and state costs.

Case 1561 starts on 2019-04-10. The original LP produces no Haukeli power. Its Haukeli water shadow is 52,414.78 EUR/Mm³ and Tokke/Vinjevatn shadow is 23,525.74–23,841.16 EUR/Mm³. Some peak hours have positive Haukeli first-segment operating margins, but their summed benefit at the full first-segment width is only 197.665 EUR. A single start/stop at the associated 0.7 fractional state costs 200.2 EUR. This explains why positive hourly margins alone do not imply profitable operation; the calculation is not a separate proof of the complete LP optimum.

The earlier suggestion that the storage derivative directly suppresses Haukeli has been corrected. At exactly the same initial reference and shared-flow correction, the proposal without that derivative produces 205.037904 MWh; adding it produces **210.270822 MWh**. Both complete solutions replay exactly. The final five-iteration schedules instead produce 128.039 and 105.135 MWh because they accept different step paths. A threshold crossing in the first arm is not restoration of the much larger dispatch observed in external method research.

A possible next numerical treatment is a prospective residual-based backtracking rule: interpolate between the current schedule and an affine-LP proposal, preserve all original linear water/PQ/state constraints by convexity, and evaluate the actual nonlinear tunnel residual before accepting a step. This remains a mathematical proposal only. It must receive a separately frozen iteration/acceptance budget before any new solve; none has been run. A flow secant forced through the origin would fix one sign problem but can double the tangent slope and worsen the large-movement overshoot, so it is not an automatic remedy.

The detailed independent derivation is `docs/provided_vinje_tunnel_equality_review.md`: for smooth, unregularized residual `R` and affine proposal direction `d`, the linear solve gives `R + J*d = 0`, so a sufficiently small step has residual `(1-alpha)*R + O(alpha²)`. This gives a local feasibility-descent rationale, with explicit caveats at clamps/regularized slopes, at zero residual, and for invalid interpolated duals. It is a proposed numerical method, not an additional experiment.
