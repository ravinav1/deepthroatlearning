This audit checks the actual **Round 5 submission, user-reported public score 0.99172**, against its code, saved models, supplied task materials and relevant research. It finds no current submission-order or feature-alignment defect. The strongest remaining concerns are shared errors in unusual operating conditions, weak switching predictions, and how historical validation is reused to choose experiments. More generic tree tuning is not the best-supported next step.

**The remaining errors are more specific than “the model does not generalize.”** Prior controlled tuning measured 0.999306 training AUC versus 0.988261 validation AUC for the separate-generator LightGBM component. Increasing to 127 leaves improved training fit and worsened validation. Other capacity, regularization and training-length changes produced no robust improvement to the actual ensemble. This supports a transfer problem, while leaving representation, rare regimes and fitting tradeoffs unresolved. [Controlled tuning report](round6_tuning_report_20261017.md).

Fresh diagnostics show that hours within two hours of a labelled switch occupy only 8.05% of outputs in 2021 and 11.87% in 2022, but wrong positive-negative pairs touching them account for 52.55% and 67.80% of ranking errors. After adjusting for how many pairs could touch those hours, the enrichment is 3.39 and 2.77 times. AUC restricted to the other hours is approximately 0.99459 and 0.99552. These are label-defined diagnostic slices, never prediction inputs; this does not justify forcing schedules to be smooth.

The problem is also broader than Lio and Haukeli. Allocating half of each wrongly ranked pair to each endpoint, the largest 2022 error shares are Lio 13.77%, Haukeli 10.57%, Hogga 10.33%, Kjela 9.39% and Vinje G3 8.85%. These allocations sum to one across all generators. Conversely, roughly 92% of errors being cross-generator pairs is **not** evidence that calibration is the main defect: about 93% of all available pairs are cross-generator. [Fresh error diagnostics](audit_20261017_diagnostics.json).

An idealized error budget reinforces this: even eliminating every wrong pair touching Lio or Haukeli while leaving all other predictions fixed would bring native 2022 only to 0.99401. Perfectly repairing both inspected November cases would add at most 0.00093. These are label-based upper bounds, not achievable model forecasts or claims about the competition years. They prevent treating either the weakest two units or a few striking cases as the entire remaining problem. [Error-budget arithmetic](audit_20261017_error_budget.json).

Two case studies expose shared mistakes across components:

| Case | Observed labels | Expected ON hours from current probabilities | Relevant supplied context |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2872, 11 November 2022 | Vesle Kjela 100; Byrte 132; Lio 98 | 0.523; 38.343; 31.905 | High starting storage and substantial incoming water, despite prices below the local terminal water value at Vesle Kjela |
| 2438, 3 September 2021 | Tokke 326 unit-hours; Vinje 174 | 628.3; 316.4 | Vinjevatn terminal water value is 0.01, while important upstream water values are about 198–219; the local reservoir is small |

Summing probabilities gives an expected count, not a binary schedule. In case 2872, the two Vesle Kjela LightGBM seeds predict only 0.020 and 0.031 ON hours; the legacy branch predicts 1.020. The problem survives blending because all branches miss it. A saved-model contribution check attributes much of its negative score to the normalized price-minus-water-value margin. That explains the fitted score, not the optimizer's causal reasoning; correlated inputs make feature attributions non-unique.

A further replay distinguishes fitting from transfer. The same pre-2021 Vesle models fit the three comparable rare 2020 **training** cases well: true ON counts are 19, 116 and 42; seed 299 predicts 16.39, 113.31 and 41.33, and seed 673 predicts 19.91, 111.85 and 40.69. Both make zero classification errors on their sampled training rows. Thus the existing representation can fit that episode. Its failure in 2022 is stronger evidence of sparse-regime extrapolation than proof that the model cannot combine the inputs at all. These three overlapping cases represent very little independent experience.

There is a concrete economic-representation difference: the 2020 episodes activate the price-scale floor of 10, leaving scaled terminal values of 0.517–0.617. The failing November 2022 episodes have scaled values of 4.526–4.753. Without the floor, the earlier ratios would be about 3.67–3.96. This makes normalization sensitivity worth examining, but does not prove the floor caused the error: other conditions differ, absolute prices affect startup economics, and the retained scale/input summaries preserve information. Changing normalization after inspecting three cases must be treated as a new controlled hypothesis, not a correction of a demonstrated bug.

Storage plus natural inflow exceeding capacity is only a **pressure proxy**. It is not proof a generator must run: large bypass/spill alternatives exist, and their destinations differ from turbine releases. The supplied files omit actual optimized flows and several teacher boundary settings. The examples support testing a better water-allocation representation; they do not establish a hard correction rule or explain the entire gap to 0.995. [Numerical cases, attribution and physical alternatives](audit_20261017_task_physics.json).

**Some temporal information is compressed, and some “new” ideas were already tried.** Current trees receive each operating constraint's current value and weekly maximum. They do not receive an explicit remaining release requirement or time to its next change. For example, the r_Vest minimum flow changes from 14 to 5 m³/s at hour 144 in case 2871 and hour 120 in case 2872. Neural members see the full constraint sequence, but their contribution varies by plant.

This needs a precise qualification: the earlier water-budget experiment already encoded remaining **direct-outlet** releases through its net-inflow arithmetic. It failed in the older pooled XGBoost comparison. The multi-source r_Vest burden was excluded, and the actual Round 5 tree branch has neither representation. Repeating the whole old budget experiment is not automatically a new idea. [Independent cross-review](audit_20261017_pipeline_crossreview.json).

The neural inputs also omit individual hourly inflows for several reservoirs, although all weekly totals are available. Much of this omission is redundant: several reservoir profiles are almost proportional, and river inflow is nearly recoverable from existing inputs. Staavatn and Venemo have more distinct timing. An all-reservoir-profile experiment was tested on an older unrescaled pooled XGBoost and had mixed results; it has not settled the question for the present models.

The two existing price percentile features—rank within the complete week and rank among remaining hours—helped XGBoost, but have never been enabled in the current separate-generator LightGBM. The failed LightGBM test used an older pooled 31-leaf model. This is an inexpensive untested interaction, not a proven gain waiting to be switched on.

An accepted Round 4 lower-learning-rate Transformer mean was also absent from the Round 5 recipe. One fixed cached combination was evaluated without fitting or searching weights. It improves 2021 by 0.0000256, native 2022 Jan 7 onward by 0.0000431 and frozen 2022 Jan 7 onward by 0.0000116. All nine paired block-bootstrap intervals—7, 14 and 28 days, 1,000 draws each—include zero. This is an experiment-integration omission, but not convincing evidence of substantial missed performance. No submission was generated from it. [Integration uncertainty](audit_20261017_round4_integration_uncertainty.json).

**Validation needs a broader, more explicit interpretation.** The temporal purge itself is correct: six daily starts whose seven-day targets cross the boundary are removed. The concern is repeated selection on the remaining years. Some inherited native-2022 components selected epochs or iterations using 2022 labels. The new Round 5 tree counts were fixed from 2019/2020, but that does not make the entire inherited ensemble independent of 2022. Frozen 2022 removes that direct checkpoint-selection route; it still reuses the same 2022 labels and is not a second independent holdout.

A 2021-only promotion gate can also miss the specific regime an experiment addresses. Positive upstream capacity pressure with every weekly price below Vesle Kjela's terminal value occurs in three consecutive pre-2021 cases, zero 2021 cases and four 2022 cases. A correction designed around that mechanism cannot be judged from 2021 alone. The remedy is a predeclared multi-year and regime-aware development comparison, not treating the known November cases as a new test set or repeatedly choosing whichever year looks favourable.

Private 2024 inputs differ from the historical validation years. Median weekly relative price standard deviation is 0.398 in 2024 versus 0.106 in the labelled data. Weeks containing any negative price number 8 in 2021, none in 2022, 90 in public 2023 and 156 in private 2024. Those are overlapping case weeks, not independent events. However, high-relative-volatility historical cases do **not** show consistently higher errors: their pair-error enrichments are 0.915, 0.967 and 0.959 in native 2021, native 2022 and frozen 2022. Therefore blanket volatility weighting is not currently supported. Test-input shift alone neither proves changed label relationships nor demonstrates a useful weighting scheme. [Input distributions](audit_20261017_input_shift.json), [historical regime check](audit_20261017_regimes.json).

The scale-floor hypothesis also has a useful negative check. The floor affects 260 labelled cases—34 in 2015 and 226 in 2020—but **zero** cases in 2021–2024. It therefore changes the representation of some training experience, not test-time clipping in the current competition inputs. The exact rare pressure/below-value conjunction discussed above is also absent from 2023/2024. Its historical failure is informative about transfer, but cannot be assumed to be the current competition bottleneck. [Independent scaling probe](audit_20261017_scaling.json).

**What the research changes in the plan.** Logistic/BCE training remains principled for estimating probabilities whose ordering is evaluated by AUC; another ranking or focal loss is not required by the metric. The relevant ranking theory uses distributional assumptions and does not guarantee finite-sample gains on these dependent cases. [Agarwal, 2014](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume15/agarwal14b/agarwal14b.pdf).

Sequential stacking is a genuine alternative to adding more raw features: a second learner uses out-of-sample predictions from nearby positions as context. For this task that means neighbouring predicted logits within the same supplied week, never true neighbouring commitment labels. Existing full-week Transformers already have temporal context, so the proposed benefit is an easier learned representation for a small correction, not access to previously unknown future data. [Cohen and Carvalho, 2005](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0378.pdf).

Importance weighting assumes stable conditional label relationships and adequate source support; estimated weights can increase variance. The fresh regime check gives no reason to prioritize it now. [Sugiyama et al., 2007](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume8/sugiyama07a/sugiyama07a.pdf), [effective sample size under covariate shift](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.01184v5).

Learning-to-optimize papers often target solver speed, feasibility or objective cost, which differ from matching this teacher's marginal labels. A recent thermal unit-commitment study expands 2,620 original instances to 420,000 **re-solved** instances; it does not justify perturbing our prices while keeping the original labels. A different feasible and profitable schedule can still score worse against the supplied labels. [Thermal UC study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21891v1), [Neural Diving](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.13349v3).

Modern tabular neural models remain optional diversity candidates. In particular, TabPFN should not be rejected using obsolete row limits: its 2026 report describes substantially larger tasks, though local compute and transfer on dependent chronological data remain open. The 2,922-case representation also has 2,352 binary targets, so it is not a standard single-target tabular-classification call. These models are not more strongly motivated than the diagnosed experiments below. [TabPFN-3 report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.13986), [full methods review with 15 primary sources](audit_20261017_methods.json).

**Recommended experiments, with stop conditions.** Keep the existing submission and tree settings as the reference. Do not launch another broad hyperparameter search.

| Order | Controlled change | What it would establish | Stop or reject when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: inexpensive | Add only the two existing temporal price ranks to the current separate-generator LightGBM; start with seed 299 and matched reference | Whether a useful price representation transfers to the actual current model | Improvement disappears in the full ensemble or reverses across historical evaluations; do not expand into a parameter search |
| 2: distinct ML method | A strongly shrunken temporal residual correction of the current two-seed LightGBM branch, with identity and same-hour-only controls | Whether neighbouring predicted states add value beyond ordinary recalibration/stacking | Temporal context fails to beat the same-hour control on total micro-AUC, even if transition accuracy improves |
| 3: grounded representation | One narrow ablation at a time: timing of the shared r_Vest burden, distinct Staavatn/Venemo inflows, or recoverable capacity-pressure arithmetic | Whether omitted within-week timing or a more transferable physical representation helps | Gains depend on the inspected rare cases, harm other years, or require treating a proxy as a hard feasibility rule |

A cached feasibility pilot for experiment 2 can use pre-2021-fitted predictions on 2021 to train the correction, choosing the first 359 weeks as a fixed cohort and applying it unchanged to 2022 Jan 7 onward. That conservative cohort choice gives 211,092 rows using the existing seed-299 stride rule; temporal context must be constructed before row thinning. Jan 7 evaluation already avoids overlap even with the last six 2021 starts, so dropping those six is not mathematically necessary at that later boundary. Correcting only the separate-generator branch reduces some legacy-checkpoint dependence, then reinserts it into the unchanged 50/50 ensemble. It requires no new base-model fits. It is still an exploratory development experiment: the overall recipe was selected using these years. If promising, justify the cost of regenerating chronological out-of-fold predictions with checkpoint budgets fixed before each fold. No clean-holdout claim should be made from the cached pilot. Neighbouring logits may help switching errors while offering little information to repair an entire week of uniformly near-zero predictions; the physical-representation hypothesis remains separate.

Existing separate-generator fits took about 145 seconds per seed/year in their recorded runs. This gives a measured basis for a small first experiment, rather than promising a two-hour search before knowing whether the change is useful. A full hydraulic optimization surrogate is a later project: first verify units, mass balance, outlet choices, terminal-value conversion and sensitivity on a few cases. The teacher is SHOP 17.17, while current SHOP documentation includes later changes in constraint timing. Exact teacher reproduction cannot be assumed from the YAML alone.
